## Note
An admin (a tenant admin or a platform admin) makes a change of rights alone. A change of rights that an admin makes with a click in the web console, after the same sign-in as for an approval there, runs at once. Its audit row says so in its main columns (`policy_rule: default:rights_change.admin_in_web_console`) and in `rights_change_run: admin_in_web_console`. A change of rights asked with a token (the command line, REST, MCP) or by an AI agent waits, and only an admin approves it, with a click in the web console. Outside MEHO's own login realm that admin may be the person who asked; the audit log records a self-approval. An admin may change their own login, for example their own passkey or group. The policy file import (`policy.apply`) and the category edit (`policy.classify`) follow the same rules. The command line and REST refuse to approve a change of rights with "This is a change of rights. Approve this change of rights in the web console." A change of rights that reads, writes or makes a credential, a `destructive` one, one with the "always wait" mark, and a `secret.move` with a `keycloak:` side always wait, also in the web console. Outside MEHO's own login realm a Keycloak write that does not change one login or group (for example a new client scope or protocol mapper, or turning a login on) always waits too. A change that can give platform admin rights still needs a platform admin. In MEHO's own login realm, only a platform admin makes a change of rights alone in the web console, after a fresh sign-in. This holds for every Keycloak write that is a change of rights there, also one that does not change one login or group. A tenant admin who is not a platform admin never makes or approves a change of rights there, also not one that sets only a first and last name: it waits, and only a platform admin approves it. A platform admin who asked in the web console may approve their own waiting change there, after a fresh sign-in. A change asked with a token, or by an agent, is never approved by the login that asked: it waits for a second platform admin. In MEHO's own login realm, MEHO first refuses the writes that would break its own sign-in, before any wait: a delete of a client MEHO signs in with, a change of such a client other than its name, description, a new address that keeps the old ones, turning it on or removing its own login flow (never on the approval clients), a new mapper on such a client, a mapper change on a client scope that such a client uses, and a realm change other than names, mail, events, languages, turning the realm on and setting the browser login flow back to `browser`. No approval lets them run, also not the approval of a second platform admin. A write that MEHO lets through and that can change the sign-in MEHO asks for before an approval (an allowed change of the approval clients, the console login client or the client of the Keycloak admin API, a new client or an update that sets the `clientId` of one of them, a mapper of a sign-in claim on another client or client scope, turning the realm on, or setting its browser login flow back to `browser`) never runs alone, and the login that asked never approves it, nor a person behind the agent that asked: a second platform admin does. A change of rights asked by an agent, an add-on or a runner may be approved by an admin who stands behind it, in the web console after a fresh sign-in, the same as their own change (in the login realm only by a platform admin); an Operator behind it never approves it. A waiting request follows these rules at the upgrade; in the login realm the login that asked a request from before the upgrade does not approve it. See https://evoila.github.io/meho/latest/guides/approvals-and-break-glass/#changes-of-rights.

## Action
Tell your admins that they approve a change of rights in the web console, not with `meho approvals approve`. Scripts that approve a change of rights on the command line or over REST must stop doing so. In MEHO's own login realm, make a delete of a client MEHO signs in with, a change of its session times or other refused settings, and a new mapper on such a client by hand in the Keycloak admin console: MEHO refuses them, and no approval lets them run. An install with one platform admin needs a second platform admin in MEHO's own login realm, to approve two kinds of writes there: a write that MEHO lets through and that can change the approval sign-in (an allowed change of the approval clients, the console login client or the client of the Keycloak admin API, a new client or an update that sets the `clientId` of one of them, a mapper of a sign-in claim on another client or client scope, turning the realm on, or setting its browser login flow back to `browser`), and every change of rights asked with a token, the command line, MCP or by an agent. Add the second platform admin before the upgrade, or make these changes by hand in the Keycloak admin console. No schema action.
