    One person in several tenants¶

    A tenant is a separate space in MEHO, with its own systems and passwords. This guide lets one person work in more than one tenant with one login. At the end:

    • the person logs in once,
    • MEHO lists all of their tenants, with their role in each,
    • they pick one tenant at a time in the web interface, on the command line or in the MCP settings, and switch without logging out.

    A site with one tenant needs to do nothing

    MEHO behaves exactly as before. Every person keeps their tenant and their role, and no request needs a tenant header. A test in CI checks this against a real Keycloak, before and after the setup adds the tenant list. See Upgrades.

    Each change waits for approval

    Putting a person in a group, or taking them out, runs at the dangerous risk level when you do it through MEHO, so each change waits for approval. Only a MEHO-SA-Operator may approve their own change; anyone else needs a second person (a MEHO-SA-Operator or an admin). The self-approval switch does not cover the dangerous risk level (decision record: tenant groups stay at the dangerous risk level).

    How it works¶

    • Every role group in Keycloak carries the attribute meho_tenant. Its value is <tenant-uuid>:<tenant>:<Role>, for example <tenant-uuid>:team-b:Operators.
    • The Keycloak mapper meho-tenants copies the values of all of the person's groups into the login token, as the list meho_tenants.
    • MEHO always works in one current tenant. A request names it in the Meho-Tenant header (a tenant name or ID). The CLI and the MCP connection helpers send this header for you. The web interface keeps the current tenant in your web session.
    • The role in the current tenant comes from the person's group in that tenant. A role in another tenant never applies.
    • A login with one tenant needs no header. A login with several tenants and no header gets 400 tenant_not_chosen, with the list of tenants.

    The design is in the decision record.

    Before you start¶

    • Your realm has the meho-tenants mapper and the meho_tenant attribute on the five groups of your first tenant. The standard setup adds them from v0.39.0 on. To add them to an older realm, run the setup again (see Upgrades).
    • MEHO v0.40.0 or later. In v0.40.0 to v0.43.0 the web interface answers tenant_not_chosen to a person in several tenants. The web interface has the tenant picker from v0.44.0 on. The CLI and MCP work from v0.40.0 on.
    • A MEHO-SA-Operator who approves their own changes, a second person who can approve, or the agent setup from Approvals and break-glass.

    Step 1: Add the tenant in Keycloak¶

    A new tenant needs a UUID and a short name. The short name uses lowercase letters, digits and dashes, for example team-b. Make the UUID once and keep it:

    uuidgen | tr 'A-Z' 'a-z'

    The tenant gets five role groups. They carry only meho_tenant, no tenant_id and no tenant_role:

    Group meho_tenant Role in MEHO
    MEHO-team-b-Readers <tenant-uuid>:team-b:Readers read_only
    MEHO-team-b-Operators <tenant-uuid>:team-b:Operators operator
    MEHO-team-b-SA-Operators <tenant-uuid>:team-b:SA-Operators operator, may approve their own changes
    MEHO-team-b-Admins <tenant-uuid>:team-b:Admins tenant_admin
    MEHO-team-b-Approvers <tenant-uuid>:team-b:Approvers read_only, may approve

    Pick the way that matches your Keycloak. The two setups are safe to run twice.

    Your own Keycloak¶

    Run the setup command with --add-tenant. Pass the same --tenant-id as on the first run: without it, the command writes the all-zero UUID to the five groups of your first tenant. If your first tenant is not called main, pass its name with --tenant-name too.

    KEYCLOAK_ADMIN_PASSWORD=... KEYCLOAK_ADMIN_USER_PASSWORD=... \
meho admin keycloak bootstrap-clients \
  --keycloak-base-url https://keycloak.example.com \
  --realm <realm> \
  --admin-username <keycloak-admin> \
  --mcp-resource-uri https://meho.example.com/mcp \
  --tenant-id <first-tenant-uuid> \
  --admin-user-username <first-admin> \
  --add-tenant team-b \
  --add-tenant-id <tenant-uuid>

    Use the same values as on your first run. The command does not reset the password of a user that exists already. Without --add-tenant-id, the command reuses the UUID the groups already carry, or makes a new one and prints it. Keep it.

    The command puts the meho-tenants mapper on meho-cli, meho-cli-approve and meho-mcp. It does not change the web client meho-web. If you use the web interface, add the mapper to meho-web by hand, with the settings in Keycloak realm setup, Step 3.

    The built-in Keycloak¶

    Add the tenant to the Helm values, then run helm upgrade. The bootstrap Job makes the five groups.

    coreKeycloak:
  tenantName: main          # the short name of your first tenant
  tenants:
    - name: team-b
      id: <tenant-uuid>

    The built-in realm already has the meho-tenants mapper on meho-cli, meho-mcp and meho-web.

    By hand¶

    Create the five groups at the top level, each with the one attribute meho_tenant from the table above. Check that the meho-tenants mapper is on every client people log in with, with Multivalued and Aggregate attribute values on. The settings are in Keycloak realm setup, Step 3.

    For every way¶

    Never set meho_tenant on a person. With Aggregate attribute values on, Keycloak adds a value on the person to their list. Keep people from editing their own attributes, as Keycloak realm setup, Step 5 says.

    Step 2: Give the tenant its own Vault login role¶

    MEHO keeps a tenant's passwords in Vault under secret/tenants/<tenant-uuid>/. The shared policies of Add a tenant, Step 6 read the tenant from the person's Vault identity, and one identity holds one tenant. So a person in two tenants needs one Vault login role per tenant. Each role can read only its own tenant's path. The rights of Step 6 do not go away on their own: Vault keeps them on the person's identity and adds them to every login, with any role. So the person also leaves the Vault groups of Step 6 (item 4), and you check each role (item 5).

    1. Write a policy for this tenant's path only. Put the tenant UUID into the path itself. A read-only example:

      # meho-tenant-team-b: the secrets of the tenant team-b only.
path "secret/data/tenants/<tenant-uuid>/*" {
  capabilities = ["read"]
}
path "secret/metadata/tenants/<tenant-uuid>/*" {
  capabilities = ["read", "list"]
}
# privileged/ holds the admin-only secrets. The two rules above
# cover it too, so refuse it here. The more exact path wins.
path "secret/data/tenants/<tenant-uuid>/privileged/*" {
  capabilities = ["deny"]
}
path "secret/metadata/tenants/<tenant-uuid>/privileged/*" {
  capabilities = ["deny"]
}

      The login role of item 2 accepts every role group of the tenant, so every member gets the same rights from this policy. Keep it at the lowest level the tenant needs. Add the write rights the tenant needs in the same way. The rights per role, and the privileged/ rule, are in the per-tenant Vault policies runbook, section 1. Use the fixed UUID where that runbook has the {{identity.entity.metadata.tenant_id}} template.

    2. Create the login role and tie it to the tenant. bound_claims with the glob type requires an entry <tenant-uuid>:<anything> in the person's meho_tenants list. Vault accepts the login when any entry of the list matches.

      vault write auth/jwt/role/meho-tenant-team-b - <<'JSON'
{
  "role_type": "jwt",
  "user_claim": "sub",
  "bound_audiences": "<keycloak-audience>",
  "bound_claims_type": "glob",
  "bound_claims": {"meho_tenants": "<tenant-uuid>:*"},
  "policies": "meho-mcp,meho-tenant-team-b",
  "token_ttl": "1h"
}
JSON

      meho-mcp is the base policy from Vault provisioning, section 3. Use your own mount path in place of jwt, and your KEYCLOAK_AUDIENCE in bound_audiences.

      Do not bind this role on the tenant_id claim. For a person in two tenants, tenant_id holds the first tenant, not the current one.

    3. Tell MEHO which role belongs to which tenant. Add <tenant-uuid>=<vault-role> to VAULT_OIDC_ROLE_BY_TENANT, through extraEnv in your Helm values. Separate the entries with commas. Then run helm upgrade.

      extraEnv:
  - name: VAULT_OIDC_ROLE_BY_TENANT
    value: "<first-tenant-uuid>=meho-tenant-main,<tenant-uuid>=meho-tenant-team-b"

      MEHO picks the role from the current tenant. A wrong entry stops MEHO from starting, so check the value before you roll it out. More about this setting is in deploying.md (row v0.35.13).

    4. Take the person out of the Vault groups of Step 6. The roles above log in with user_claim: sub on the same auth mount as the shared role. So every login of the person, with any role, lands on the same Vault identity. Vault adds the policies of that identity's groups to each login. A person who is still in a group meho-tenant-<first-tenant-uuid>-<role> can then read the first tenant's secrets through the team-b role too, also after you take them out of the first tenant's Keycloak group. So give every tenant the person works in its own role (item 3), and take the person out of every group of Step 6. Read the group, then write it again with the other members only. The member list replaces the old one.

      meho operation call vault-1.x vault.identity.group.read --target vault-main \
  --params '{"name": "meho-tenant-<first-tenant-uuid>-read_only"}'
meho operation call vault-1.x vault.identity.group.write --target vault-main \
  --params '{"name": "meho-tenant-<first-tenant-uuid>-read_only", "policies": ["meho-tenant-read-only"], "member_entity_ids": ["<entity-id of each other member>"]}'

      Each write waits for the second person. The tenant_id metadata on the identity may stay: without the groups, no policy uses it.

    5. Check that each role stops at its own tenant. Get the person's Keycloak token as in the connector Vault policy runbook, section 4. Log in to Vault with it against each role, and ask Vault what the token may do. deny means no access.

      TOKEN_B=$(vault write -field=token auth/jwt/login role=meho-tenant-team-b jwt="$PERSON_JWT")
vault token capabilities "$TOKEN_B" "secret/data/tenants/<tenant-uuid>/<target>"             # expect: read
vault token capabilities "$TOKEN_B" "secret/data/tenants/<first-tenant-uuid>/<target>"       # expect: deny
vault token capabilities "$TOKEN_B" "secret/data/tenants/<tenant-uuid>/privileged/<target>"  # expect: deny

      Then do the same with meho-tenant-main, with the two tenants swapped. A read on the other tenant's path means the person is still in a Vault group of that tenant (item 4), or the policy has the wrong UUID (item 1).

    Agent, service and runner logins have no meho_tenants list, so Vault refuses them for a role that is bound only on meho_tenants. Plan their Vault access before you add the tenant to VAULT_OIDC_ROLE_BY_TENANT.

    Step 3: Put a person in two tenants¶

    Put the person in one role group per tenant. For example, they read in your first tenant and operate in team-b:

    meho keycloak group member add --target keycloak-main \
  --username alice@example.com --group-name MEHO-Readers
meho keycloak group member add --target keycloak-main \
  --username alice@example.com --group-name MEHO-team-b-Operators

    Each command waits for approval. The second person approves it:

    meho approvals list
meho approvals approve <approval-id>

    The rules:

    • One role group per tenant. Two groups of the same tenant: MEHO refuses the login with 401 multiple_role_groups and names the groups.
    • Groups of two different tenants are fine.
    • A person may also be only in the groups of an extra tenant, for example only in MEHO-team-b-Admins. MEHO reads their tenant from the list.

    The person logs in again (or waits for the next token refresh). Then they check their tenants:

    meho tenants use

    The command lists each tenant with the role in it, and marks the current one.

    Step 4: Switch tenants¶

    The person picks the current tenant. No new login is needed: one token holds all of their tenants.

    Web interface. After the login, a person with several tenants sees Choose a tenant first. A person with one tenant goes straight on. For a person with several tenants, and for a platform admin, the tenant box in the sidebar shows Switch and opens the same page (/ui/tenant). The account page lists the person's tenants with the role in each. A switch is stored in the audit log ("tenant switched from A to B") and opens the dashboard. A page that was open in another browser tab before the switch refuses to send: "The tenant changed. Reload the page."

    Command line.

    meho tenants use team-b      # a tenant name or ID
meho status                  # shows the current tenant

    The choice is stored in config.json, next to the backplane address. Every command then sends it. The variable MEHO_TENANT wins over the stored choice for one shell:

    MEHO_TENANT=team-b meho targets list

    MCP (Claude Code, Claude Desktop). One MCP session works in one tenant from start to end. Set the tenant before the session starts:

    • Claude Code plugin: MEHO_TENANT="team-b" in your shell profile or in the plugin config file (Claude Code).
    • Claude Desktop bundle: the Tenant field in the extension settings (Claude Desktop).

    To switch, change the setting and start a new session. The first session in a tenant opens a browser login once more. Without a tenant, a session of a person in several tenants may only call meho_status, which lists their tenants. No MCP tool switches the tenant inside a session. More: Which tenant a session works in.

    The platform admin role¶

    The group MEHO-Platform-Admins gives rights across all tenants. It is not a role group: it holds no tenant and no role. So a platform admin is also in at least one role group.

    A platform admin may:

    • work in any tenant that exists in MEHO, also without a group there. MEHO knows a tenant after the first request of a person in it. In the web interface, the tenant picker lists every tenant. On the command line, use MEHO_TENANT=<tenant-uuid>: meho tenants use accepts only the tenants of the person's own groups.
    • act there as tenant_admin. When the person has a role group in that tenant, the role of that group applies instead.
    • use the cross-tenant options, for example --tenant on the commands that have it. For a tenant where the person has a role group, MEHO answers 403 tenant_select_with_header: pick that tenant with MEHO_TENANT or meho tenants use, so the role of that group applies.

    A platform admin may not:

    • approve their own changes. Only the SA-Operators group of the current tenant allows that.
    • get the approver flag from the platform admin group. It comes only from an Approvers group of the current tenant.
    • pick a tenant that does not exist. MEHO answers 403 tenant_not_found and never makes a tenant from a typo.
    • move a machine login. Agent, service and runner logins always stay in their own tenant, also with --tenant and also when their token carries the platform admin claim.

    Every audit row of a request where the role came from the platform admin group carries tenant_role_source: platform_admin.

    When a change takes effect¶

    Keycloak builds the tenant list again at each token refresh. So:

    • A new group shows at the next token refresh, at the latest at the next login.
    • A removed group ends at the next token refresh, at the latest at the next login. Until then the person still works in that tenant.

    What the person sees after a removal:

    • Web interface: "You are no longer in the tenant you worked in." MEHO moves to the one tenant left, or shows the tenant picker.
    • Command line: the stored tenant is refused with 403 tenant_not_member. meho status explains it. Pick another tenant with meho tenants use.
    • MCP: a new session in that tenant is refused with tenant_not_member.

    If something does not work¶

    What you see Cause Fix
    tenant_not_chosen The login is in several tenants and the request named none. meho tenants use <name>, or set the tenant in the MCP settings.
    tenant_not_member The tenant you chose is not in your list, or you were taken out of it. meho tenants use lists your tenants. Ask your admin for a role group in the tenant.
    multiple_role_groups … of the same tenant … Two role groups of one tenant. Keep one group per tenant, then log in again.
    meho tenants use lists no tenants, or only one The realm has no meho-tenants mapper, or you did not log in again after the group change. Step 1 (the mapper), then log in again.
    Vault refuses the login in the new tenant The Vault role is missing, its bound_claims do not match, or VAULT_OIDC_ROLE_BY_TENANT has no entry for the tenant. Step 2.
    A person reads the first tenant's secrets through the second tenant's Vault role The person is still in a Vault group of the first tenant. Vault adds the group's rights to every login of the person. Step 2, item 4. Then check with item 5.

    More answers are in Troubleshooting auth walls.
